Main textbook

    Publisher record and DOI.

    Main source used for these course notes. The chapter guide below connects the book to each Theory module.

Rotations and projections

  1. Steve Marschner and Peter Shirley. Fundamentals of Computer Graphics. 5th ed. A K Peters/CRC Press, 2021.

    Publisher and contents.

    Chapters 7–8 cover transformation matrices and viewing. Use these for further study of homogeneous transformations, perspective and orthographic viewing.

  2. Kevin M. Lynch and Frank C. Park. Modern Robotics: Mechanics, Planning, and Control. 1st ed. Cambridge University Press, 2017.

    Publisher and DOI · Authors’ university page and free preprint.

    Sections 3.2.1, 3.2.3 and 3.3.1 cover rotation matrices, exponential coordinates and homogeneous transformations; Appendix B covers Euler angles and unit quaternions. The authors’ updated 2019 preprint is a version of the first edition.

Curves and surfaces

  1. Andrew Pressley. Elementary Differential Geometry. 2nd ed. Springer, London, 2010.

    Publisher and DOI.

    Read §§1.2, 2.1 and 2.3 for arc length, curvature and space curves; Chapters 6–8 for fundamental forms and surface curvature; §10.2 for Theorema Egregium; and Chapter 13 for Gauss–Bonnet.

  2. Gerald Farin. Curves and Surfaces for CAGD: A Practical Guide. 5th ed. Morgan Kaufmann, 2002.

    Publisher and DOI.

    Chapters 4–5 develop de Casteljau’s algorithm and the Bernstein–Bézier form; Chapter 13 treats rational curves; Chapter 14 tensor-product patches; Chapter 20 surface geometric continuity; and Chapter 22 Coons patches.

  3. Les Piegl and Wayne Tiller. The NURBS Book. 2nd ed. Springer-Verlag, Berlin Heidelberg, 1997.

    Publisher and DOI.

    A detailed algorithmic reference for B-splines and NURBS. See pp. 117–139 for rational curves and surfaces, pp. 141–228 for geometric algorithms, and pp. 281–331 for conics and circles.

  4. C.-K. Shene. CS3621 Introduction to Computing with Geometry Notes. Michigan Technological University, n.d.

    Free university notes. Accessed 8 September 2026.

    Learning source identified by the course author: The Winged-Edge Data Structure. The author also identified Rational Bézier Curves: Conic Sections as a useful related page. Further worked explanations: Bézier subdivision, degree elevation, rational Bézier curves, circular arcs, and B-spline surfaces.

  5. Steven A. Coons. Surfaces for Computer-Aided Design of Space Forms. Technical Report MAC-TR-41. Project MAC, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, June 1967.

    Original report, free PDF from MIT.

    An original source for boundary-defined surface patches. See Section III for the surface equation, §3.6 for blending functions, and §5.2 for slope continuity between adjacent patches.

B-splines

  1. Carl de Boor. A Practical Guide to Splines. Revised ed. Applied Mathematical Sciences 27. Springer, New York, 2001. ISBN 978-0-387-95366-3.

    Publisher · Author’s page confirming the revised edition.

    A foundation for spline spaces, B-spline representations and numerical evaluation. Use the revised 2001 edition when following this citation.

  2. M. G. Cox. “The Numerical Evaluation of B-Splines.” IMA Journal of Applied Mathematics 10(2), 134–149, 1972.

    Original paper: publisher and DOI.

    An original reference for the recurrence-based evaluation of B-splines associated with the Cox–de Boor recursion.

  3. Carl de Boor. “On Calculating with B-Splines.” Journal of Approximation Theory 6(1), 50–62, 1972.

    Original paper: publisher and DOI.

    An original reference for B-spline computation, complementing Cox’s paper and the divided-difference treatment in these notes.

  4. I. J. Schoenberg. Cardinal Spline Interpolation. CBMS-NSF Regional Conference Series in Applied Mathematics 12. SIAM, Philadelphia, 1973.

    Publisher and DOI.

    Further study of uniform/cardinal B-splines: Chapter 2 treats their basis property; Chapter 4, §6 applies cardinal interpolation results to periodic splines.

  5. Carl de Boor. “Divided Differences.” Surveys in Approximation Theory 1, 46–69, 2005.

    Journal page and free paper.

    A focused reference for divided differences, including repeated arguments, useful alongside the derivation of B-spline basis functions.

Solid modeling

  1. Martti Mäntylä. An Introduction to Solid Modeling. Computer Science Press, College Park, Maryland, 1988. ISBN 0-88175-108-1.

    Author’s institutional publication record.

    Further reading on boundary representations, topology, data structures and Euler operators.

  2. Aristides A. G. Requicha. “Representations for Rigid Solids: Theory, Methods, and Systems.” ACM Computing Surveys 12(4), 437–464, December 1980.

    Publisher and DOI.

    A foundational survey comparing representations of solids, including boundary representation and constructive solid geometry.

  3. Bruce G. Baumgart. Winged Edge Polyhedron Representation. Stanford Artificial Intelligence Project Memo AIM-179; Computer Science Department Report CS-320. Stanford University, October 1972.

    Original Stanford report, free PDF.

    Section II introduces the winged-edge data structure; §III.A discusses Euler primitives.

  4. Lutz Kettner. “Halfedge Data Structures.” CGAL User Manual. Also: CGAL contributors, “Euler Operations.”

    Official halfedge manual · Official Euler-operations reference. Accessed 8 September 2026.

    Implementation-oriented reading on incidence links, traversals and mesh operations. CGAL’s conventions can differ from the simplified Python examples in these notes.

  5. OpenSCAD contributors. OpenSCAD User Manual and Language Reference. Continuously maintained documentation.

    Official documentation index. Accessed 8 September 2026.

    Use the linked language reference for primitive solids, transformations, union, difference and intersection, and for checking the syntax of the OpenSCAD examples.

CAD software and data exchange

  1. FreeCAD Project Association. FreeCAD Features and File Format Documentation.

    Official features overview · FCStd format. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Official descriptions of FreeCAD’s parametric B-Rep geometry, workbench and Python architecture, native documents, and supported exchange families.

  2. SolveSpace contributors. SolveSpace Overview, Reference Manual, and Solver Library.

    Official overview · Reference manual and export guidance · Constraint-solver library. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Primary documentation for SolveSpace’s constraint-based modeling, linkage analysis, exact STEP export, 2D export, and reusable solver.

  3. ParaView contributors and Kitware. ParaView User’s Guide and product documentation.

    Official introduction · Readers, filters, scripting, and platform features. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Describes ParaView as an open-source scientific data-analysis and visualization pipeline for local, remote, parallel, Python, and in-situ workflows.

  4. Christophe Geuzaine, Jean-François Remacle, and Gmsh contributors. Gmsh Reference Manual.

    Official project overview · Reference manual. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Primary documentation for Gmsh geometry kernels, finite-element meshing, physical groups, .geo scripting, command-line use, APIs, and file formats.

  5. Autodesk. Inventor Product and Translator Documentation.

    Inventor overview · Saving and exporting data · Translator versions. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Use the documentation for the installed Inventor release because supported translator versions and options can change.

  6. Autodesk. AutoCAD, DWG, and DXF Documentation.

    AutoCAD overview · DWG overview · DXF reference · DWG release compatibility. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Official sources for AutoCAD’s drawing role, DWG as its native database, the DXF interchange representation, and release compatibility.

  7. Autodesk. Autodesk Fusion Product, Format, and API Documentation.

    Fusion overview · Supported formats · Fusion API. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Describes Fusion’s integrated design, manufacturing, simulation, electronics, data, collaboration, translator, and automation capabilities.

  8. Dassault Systèmes SOLIDWORKS. SOLIDWORKS Design and Translator Help.

    Modeling fundamentals · Import/export version information · API guide. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Official references for associative parts, assemblies and drawings, supported translators, and macro/add-in development.

  9. Dassault Systèmes. CATIA Product and Systems Engineering Overview.

    CATIA product family · Systems engineering · Product documentation portal. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    CATIA covers several generations, roles, and deployments; use the documentation for the exact environment when specifying native data or translators.

  10. PTC and Siemens Digital Industries Software. Representative Cloud-Native and Enterprise CAD Documentation.

    Onshape enterprise and cloud-native product development · Onshape supported formats · Siemens NX CAD. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Examples of browser-based document/version workflows and enterprise mechanical design beyond the software profiled individually.

  11. International Organization for Standardization. ISO 10303-242:2025, Industrial automation systems and integration — Product data representation and exchange — Part 242: Managed model-based 3D engineering.

    Official standard record and scope.

    STEP AP242 covers managed mechanical product data, including parts, assemblies, configuration and selected model-based engineering information. Implemented subsets vary by translator.

  12. U.S. Product Data Association. Initial Graphics Exchange Specification (IGES) 5.3.

    Specification record and PDF.

    The final ANSI-approved IGES specification for exchanging geometric, topological, annotation, and other product-definition data. IGES remains relevant in legacy curve and surface workflows.

  13. 3MF Consortium. 3D Manufacturing Format Specification Suite.

    Official specifications. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Defines a packaged mesh representation with units and extensions for additive-manufacturing information such as materials, production data, slices, and other capabilities.

  14. Siemens Digital Industries Software. Parasolid and JT Open Technology Documentation.

    Parasolid data access and translation · JT Open. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Parasolid XT is kernel-oriented exact geometry exchange; JT supports lightweight product visualization and can use different combinations of tessellation, precise geometry, structure, and PMI.

  15. Khronos Group. glTF 2.0 Specification.

    Official overview · Specification. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    An API-neutral runtime 3D asset-delivery format for scenes, meshes, materials, textures, and animation; it is complementary to CAD authoring formats.

CAD workflow, GD&T, PMI, and MBD

  1. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. NASA Systems Engineering Handbook Resources.

    System design processes · Verification and validation distinction. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Authoritative systems-engineering guidance used to frame requirements, design solution definition, verification, and validation as related lifecycle processes.

  2. International Organization for Standardization. ISO 10007:2017, Quality management — Guidelines for configuration management.

    Official standard record.

    Guidance for identifying and controlling product configuration information and changes across the lifecycle.

  3. ASME. ASME Y14.5-2018 (R2024), Dimensioning and Tolerancing.

    Official standard page.

    The authoritative ASME source for the symbols, rules, definitions, and practices of geometric dimensioning and tolerancing. The teaching example in the CAD Workflow module is simplified and not a substitute for the standard.

  4. International Organization for Standardization. ISO GPS Standards for Geometrical Tolerancing and Datums.

    ISO 1101:2017 · ISO 5459:2024.

    Official records for geometrical specification and datum/datum-system principles in the ISO GPS framework. ISO and ASME rules should not be treated as interchangeable.

  5. U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology. PMI Validation and CAD/PMI Test Resources.

    PMI validation and conformance testing · STEP File Analyzer and Viewer. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Resources for distinguishing visible PMI presentation from structured, associated semantic PMI and for checking STEP product data.

  6. ASME, ISO, and NIST. Digital Product Definition and Model-Based Definition Guidance.

    ASME Y14.41 · ISO 16792:2021 · NIST MBD publication.

    Standards and supporting research for digital product-definition data practices and model-based definition.

  7. U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology. Digital Thread for Manufacturing.

    Official program page. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Research and infrastructure for exchanging, linking, and reusing lifecycle manufacturing information.

  8. Model-Based Enterprise Interoperability Forum and U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology. Model-Based Interoperability Recommended Practices and Test Models.

    MBx-IF recommended practices · NIST CAD/PMI test models. Accessed 17 September 2026.

    Implementation practices and benchmark datasets for testing geometry, assembly, validation-property, and PMI exchange.

Topology and the Euler characteristic

  1. Kenji Ueno, Koji Shiga, and Shigeyuki Morita. A Mathematical Gift, I: The Interplay between Topology, Functions, Geometry, and Algebra. Translated by Eiko Tyler. Mathematical World, vol. 19. American Mathematical Society, 2003. ISBN 978-0-8218-3282-0.

    Book review and bibliographic record.

    Chapter 1 develops the Euler characteristic through planar triangulations, deformation, the sphere, and the torus. The interactive Euler Characteristic Proof Lab is an original teaching adaptation of that proof sequence.

Using these references

  • Keep the notes’ conventions: Rotation order, row versus column vectors, spline order versus degree, and surface-normal orientation can differ across books. Compare definitions before substituting a formula. For example, the Euler-angle extraction in these notes uses an x–y–z matrix product, while the example in Lynch and Park’s Appendix B uses z–y–x.
  • Find the full work: DOI and publisher links identify the cited edition or paper. Some require library access; free author copies, university notes and official documentation are labelled above.
  • Credit what you use: Cite the work and section you actually consulted when adapting a derivation, example or illustration. This bibliography does not change the ownership or licensing of third-party works.
