A CAD system is part of a larger engineering toolchain. A model may move from authoring to simulation, manufacturing, inspection, visualization, archiving, and service. The most useful question is therefore not “Which package is best?” but “Which representation and workflow preserve the information needed next?”

The CAD Software Ecosystem

Programs commonly grouped under “CAD software” can play very different roles. A mechanical authoring system creates constrained parts and assemblies. A drafting system organizes drawing entities and documentation. A mesher converts geometry into finite elements. A post-processor interprets numerical fields. A lightweight viewer makes a large product structure easier to review. Confusing these roles leads to poor comparisons and unsuitable exchange formats.

Key idea

Compare systems by modeling paradigm, intended lifecycle role, product structure, documentation, automation, platform, and interoperability—not by the length of a feature list.

Software Profiles

The profiles below emphasize modeling approach, suitable work, and interoperability. Licensing plans and feature bundles are intentionally omitted because they change more quickly than the underlying workflow concepts.

FreeCAD

FreeCAD is a community-developed, open-source parametric modeler built around an Open CASCADE geometry engine. Its workbench architecture covers part design, drafting, BIM, CAM, finite-element preparation, and other domains, while an integrated Python environment supports macros and custom workbenches. The native .FCStd document can retain the parametric dependency structure; neutral or mesh exports retain less.

Best suited to
Learning, customizable engineering workflows, parametric parts, and open toolchains.
Exchange
STEP and IGES for exact geometry; DXF/SVG for 2D; STL/OBJ for meshes; IFC for BIM workflows.
Check
Workbench and importer capabilities vary. A mesh import does not reconstruct features or constraints.

Official overview and formats: FreeCAD [19].

SolveSpace

SolveSpace is a compact, open-source parametric 2D/3D CAD application centered on geometric constraints. It supports constrained sketches, extrusion and revolution, Boolean operations, and planar or spatial linkage analysis. Its narrow scope makes the solver and degrees of freedom especially visible to a learner.

Best suited to
Constraint learning, small mechanical parts, linkages, and quick exact models.
Exchange
STEP for exact NURBS surfaces; DXF/SVG/PDF for 2D; STL/OBJ for tessellated output.
Check
Assembly, drawing, and lifecycle-management functions are lighter than in large MCAD platforms.

Official overview, solver, and export reference: SolveSpace [20].

OpenSCAD

OpenSCAD describes a model with a program rather than an interactive feature tree. Primitives, transformations, Boolean operations, modules, loops, and parameters make a CSG construction reproducible and easy to generate in families. This is excellent for explicit design logic, but a received triangle mesh does not recreate the source program.

Best suited to
Code-parametric components, configurable families, fixtures, and additive designs.
Exchange
STL/3MF/OFF/AMF for 3D meshes and DXF/SVG for 2D profiles.
Check
Curved geometry becomes tessellated at render/export; resolution affects size and smoothness.

See the OpenSCAD construction example in Solid Modeling. Official language documentation: [18].

ParaView

ParaView is an open-source scientific visualization and data-analysis application, not primarily a production CAD authoring system. Readers load simulation or experimental datasets, filters derive quantities or subsets, and views render geometry and fields. The pipeline can run locally, remotely, in parallel, in batch with Python, or in situ through Catalyst.

Best suited to
CFD/FEA post-processing, large datasets, fields, time histories, and reproducible visualization pipelines.
Exchange
VTK-family formats, CGNS, Exodus, OpenFOAM, XDMF, and many solver-oriented readers.
Check
A saved state may reference external datasets; derived filters can change the representation.

Official user guide and feature list: ParaView [21].

Gmsh

Gmsh is an open-source finite-element mesh generator with geometry, mesh, solver-interface, and post-processing modules. Models can be built through the graphical interface, .geo scripts, the command line, or APIs for several programming languages. It supports both built-in geometry and OpenCASCADE CAD entities.

Best suited to
Controlled FEM/CFD meshing, physical groups, parametric mesh studies, and solver preparation.
Exchange
STEP/IGES/BREP through OpenCASCADE, STL and discrete meshes, and the native MSH family.
Check
Meshing need not preserve feature history, assembly semantics, labels, colors, or PMI.

Official overview and reference manual: Gmsh [22].

Autodesk Inventor

Inventor is a mechanical-design system for parametric and direct modeling, assemblies, sheet metal, drawings, simulation, and model-based definition. Native parts, assemblies, drawings, and presentations are stored separately and resolved as a managed product structure. iLogic and the application API support rules-based automation.

Best suited to
Mechanical equipment, detailed assemblies, drawings, sheet metal, and rules-driven variants.
Exchange
STEP, IGES, JT, Parasolid, SAT, STL, OBJ, CATIA V5, DWG, DXF, and 3D PDF.
Check
Referenced files, release versions, STEP protocol, and PMI export options must be controlled.

Official product and translator documentation: Autodesk [23].

AutoCAD

AutoCAD is a general design and documentation system with a strong 2D drafting foundation and additional 3D solid and surface tools. DWG is its native drawing database; DXF is a documented interchange representation of drawing entities. Its broad use across disciplines makes layers, units, external references, fonts, and discipline-specific objects important parts of an exchange.

Best suited to
Precise drawings, layouts, annotation, standards-based documentation, and multidisciplinary 2D coordination.
Exchange
DWG for native fidelity, DXF for entity interchange, plus DWF/PDF and 3D formats where applicable.
Check
DWG release, custom vertical objects, object enablers, external references, fonts, and plot styles.

Official overview, DWG, and DXF documentation: Autodesk [24].

Autodesk Fusion

Fusion combines parametric and direct CAD with CAM, CAE, electronics, collaboration, and managed project data. Its connected environment can reduce handoffs between design and manufacturing activities, while APIs support repeatable automation. The cloud-managed data model differs from a folder of independently copied native files.

Best suited to
Integrated product development, prototyping, machining, additive preparation, and collaborative small teams.
Exchange
Imports include STEP, IGES, JT, and Parasolid; common exports include STEP, SAT/SMT, STL, 3MF, DWG, and DXF, depending on the active workflow and entitlement.
Check
Translator direction matters: a listed import format is not automatically exportable. Translated data also does not reproduce all source history.

Official overview, format, and API documentation: Autodesk [25].

SOLIDWORKS

SOLIDWORKS is a feature-parametric mechanical CAD environment for associative parts, assemblies, configurations, and drawings. Its ecosystem extends into simulation, manufacturing, data management, and model-based definition. Macros and add-ins can automate repeated design and documentation tasks.

Best suited to
Mainstream mechanical product development, assemblies, configurations, and manufacturing drawings.
Exchange
Parasolid, STEP, IGES, SAT, STL, 3MF, DXF, DWG, IFC, and direct CAD translators.
Check
Native version and references matter. Parasolid or STEP does not carry the original feature tree and mates.

Official fundamentals and translator documentation: SOLIDWORKS [26].

CATIA

CATIA is a broad product family spanning mechanical design, advanced surfacing and styling, knowledge-based engineering, systems engineering, simulation, and collaborative product development. It is widely associated with complex products, large assemblies, and high-quality surfaces. “CATIA” can refer to different generations, roles, and platform deployments, so exchange requirements must name the actual environment.

Best suited to
Aerospace, automotive, industrial equipment, complex products, Class-A surfacing, and systems contexts.
Exchange
Native CATIA part/product data, 3DXML, STEP, IGES, and JT depending on role and configuration.
Check
Constraints, knowledgeware, configurations, product links, and PMI may be lost in neutral translation.

Official CATIA product and engineering overview: Dassault Systèmes [27].

Other Important Systems

Siemens NX represents enterprise mechanical design with manufacturing, simulation, and product-lifecycle integration. Onshape provides a cloud-native document, version, branch, and history model with built-in collaboration. These systems illustrate why deployment, configuration control, collaboration, and lifecycle context belong in a comparison alongside geometric features.

Lesson
First classify the role and representation; only then compare systems in the same category.
Enterprise concern
PDM/PLM, configuration, permissions, identifiers, and lifecycle state can matter as much as geometric tools.
Exchange concern
Direct translators can preserve more than neutral files, but version support and licensing must be verified.

Official material for cloud-native CAD and enterprise design: [28].

Native and Neutral Data

A native file follows the internal schema of its authoring application. It can preserve features, parameters, constraints, assembly mates, configurations, drawing links, and application metadata. Native exchange is therefore rich but strongly dependent on product version, licensed options, referenced files, and the receiving application.

A neutral file follows a shared specification intended for more than one system. Neutral exchange improves portability but translates the model into a common information model. Even a successful solid transfer normally loses the source application’s construction history and behavioral intent.

Representation, not just extension

Two files with the same extension can carry different content. A JT file may contain only tessellation or may also include precise B-Rep and PMI. A STEP file can use different application protocols and exporter options. Record the delivery profile, not merely the suffix.

Validating an Exchange

Opening without an error proves only that the receiver parsed something. A defensible exchange checks the delivered information against the intended downstream use.

  1. Agree on units, global coordinates, geometry tolerance, format version, and STEP application protocol before export.

  2. Package referenced components, external references, textures, drawings, and manifests where the workflow requires them.

  3. Open the delivered file in the receiving application, not merely back in the source application.

  4. Compare body and component counts, bounding box, volume, area, mass properties, centroid, and assembly transforms.

  5. Inspect holes, fillets, thin features, gaps, free edges, face normals, self-intersections, and watertightness.

  6. Check names, identifiers, materials, colors, layers, configuration, PMI, datum systems, and annotation associations.

  7. For meshes, record triangle count and chordal/angular tolerance; for drawings, inspect line types, fonts, scales, and layouts.

  8. Retain the governed source model together with the derivative, export options, translator versions, warnings, and validation result.

Summary

CAD tools occupy different places in an engineering workflow. FreeCAD, SolveSpace, OpenSCAD, Inventor, Fusion, SOLIDWORKS, and CATIA author different kinds of design intent; AutoCAD emphasizes precise drawing and documentation; Gmsh prepares meshes; ParaView analyzes and visualizes simulation data. A useful comparison begins by classifying those roles.

Data exchange is a change of representation. Native data is rich but application-dependent. STEP and Parasolid can carry exact solids; IGES is still encountered for legacy surface exchange; STL and 3MF are mesh-oriented; DXF and DWG serve drawing workflows; JT and glTF serve lightweight visualization. The correct format is the one that preserves the information needed by the receiver—and every consequential transfer must be validated.
