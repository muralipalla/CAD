Solid Modeling · Topology detour

Euler characteristic: from a planar disk to genus

Follow the same quantity, \(\chi=V-E+F\), through a triangulated disk, a sphere, a torus, and connected sums. The controls distinguish geometric deformation, boundary identification, handles, and punctures.

Planar partitionsSphereTorusGenus and connected sum

Proof sequence adapted into original interactive figures from Ueno, Shiga, and Morita [42], Chapter 1, especially §§1.1–1.4.

Figure 1 · A disk

Partition a planar region into triangles

\(\chi=1\)

The attachment rule

A single triangle has \(V=3\), \(E=3\), and \(F=1\), so \(V-E+F=1\). Attach a new triangle along exactly one complete boundary edge. The attachment introduces one vertex, two edges, and one face:

\[\Delta(V,E,F)=(1,2,1),\qquad \Delta\chi=1-2+1=0.\]

For a square or rectangle cut by one diagonal, \((V,E,F)=(4,5,2)\), hence \(4-5+2=1\). A circle or ellipse can use eight curved boundary edges and five interior diagonals, with no special center vertex: \((V,E,F)=(8,13,6)\), so \(8-13+6=1\).

Why the random partitions still work

The selected boundary starts with a conforming triangulation. Each random refinement places a vertex inside one triangle, then joins it to that triangle’s three corners. This replaces one triangle by three smaller triangles—three arise simply because a triangle has three corners. The change is \(\Delta(V,E,F)=(1,3,2)\), so \(\Delta\chi=0\).

Triangle, rectangle, circle, and ellipse are all disks topologically. Drag a gold vertex or a violet diamond handle: straight or curved geometry changes, but the incidence data does not. One shared handle controls an interior edge for both neighboring faces.

Preparing the partition lab…

Remove and restore triangles. Click a triangle to remove it; click the same empty region again to restore it. An edge disappears only when no remaining triangle uses it, and a vertex disappears only when no remaining face contains it. Shared edges and vertices stay visible. The live \(V\), \(E\), and \(F\) counts therefore describe exactly the remaining triangular complex.

Figure 2 · A closed surface

Open a sphere and flatten it

\(\chi=2\)

The indexed icosphere below has \(V=42\), \(E=120\), and \(F=80\), so \(42-120+80=2\). Remove the interior of one triangle but retain its three boundary edges. The remaining triangulated surface is a disk with \(F=79\), and therefore \(42-120+79=1\).

The controls separate the topological edit from the geometric deformation. First remove one triangle; then apply a Schlegel projection so the punctured sphere opens through that missing face and becomes a crossing-free planar triangulation. No vertex, edge, or remaining face is created or deleted during flattening. Replacing the missing face adds one to \(F\), giving the closed sphere \(\chi=2\).

Figure 3 · A quotient surface

Roll a rectangle into a torus

\(\chi=0\)

The planar diagram

The rectangle carries a \(2\times2\) grid with one diagonal in every cell. Before any boundary is identified it has \((V,E,F)=(9,16,8)\), so it is a disk with \(\chi=1\). Matching colors, labels, and arrow directions show which opposite sides will be identified.

The two identifications

Join the first edge pair to make a cylinder: \((6,14,8)\), so \(\chi=0\). Bend the cylinder and join the remaining pair: \((4,12,8)\), so the torus has \(\chi=0\). In boundary notation the fundamental polygon is \(aba^{-1}b^{-1}\).

Figure 4 · Handles and punctures

Determine genus with Euler characteristic

\(\chi=2-2g-b\)

Genus and boundary components

For a compact, connected, orientable surface \(\Sigma_{g,b}\), the genus \(g\) counts handles and \(b\) counts boundary loops. Its Euler characteristic is

\[\chi(\Sigma_{g,b})=2-2g-b,\qquad g=\frac{2-b-\chi}{2}.\]

A through-tunnel in the boundary of a solid adds a handle but does not create a surface boundary: the tunnel wall joins the two openings, so the resulting B-Rep boundary remains closed and \(b=0\). Removing the interior of a disk from a surface creates one boundary component and lowers \(\chi\) by one.

Connected sum \(\#\)

To form \(M\#N\), remove one open disk from each closed surface and glue the two resulting boundary circles together. The temporary boundary circles disappear in the gluing, and

\[\chi(M\#N)=\chi(M)+\chi(N)-2.\]

Because \(\chi(T^2)=0\), taking a connected sum with a torus adds one handle: \(g(M\#T^2)=g(M)+1\). Thus a closed genus-\(g\) orientable surface is \(S^2\) when \(g=0\), and a connected sum of \(g\) tori when \(g\ge1\).

OpenSCAD example: drill handles through a sphere

The code below preserves the intent of your example while making the Boolean operation explicit. In OpenSCAD, prefixing the sphere with % makes it a background preview object, so remove that modifier when the sphere must be the first solid in difference().

$fn = 100;

module through_tunnel(angle = 0) {
  rotate([0, 0, angle])
    translate([0, -30, 0])
      rotate([90, 0, 0])
        linear_extrude(height = 120, center = true)
          circle(r = 10);
}

module toroidal_cutter() {
  rotate_extrude(angle = 360, convexity = 2)
    translate([30, 0, 0])
      circle(r = 10);
}

difference() {
  sphere(r = 50);
  through_tunnel(0);
  through_tunnel(60);
  // toroidal_cutter(); // Optional: inspect its intersection carefully.
}

Topology check. A disjoint cutter that passes completely through the ball adds a handle to its boundary surface. A toroidal cutter wholly enclosed inside the ball instead creates an internal closed cavity shell; that is a separate boundary component of the solid, not a boundary loop \(b\) of one connected surface. Always inspect connectivity before inferring genus from a Boolean feature.

Why Euler’s formula is useful. For a connected orientable mesh, first count its boundary loops \(b\), then compute \(\chi=V-E+F\). If \(2-b-\chi\) is a nonnegative even integer, \(g=(2-b-\chi)/2\). A fractional or negative answer signals that an assumption—or the mesh topology—must be checked.

What these counts mean in CAD

Here \(V\), \(E\), and \(F\) count the cells of the displayed triangulation or cell decomposition. They are not automatically the raw vertex, edge, and face totals reported by every CAD kernel: trimmed faces, inner loops, multiple shells, and non-manifold entities require the appropriate extended Euler–Poincaré relation.

Source and further reading: Kenji Ueno, Koji Shiga, and Shigeyuki Morita, A Mathematical Gift, I: The Interplay between Topology, Functions, Geometry, and Algebra [42]. The prose, counts, code, and interactive figures here are an original teaching adaptation.
