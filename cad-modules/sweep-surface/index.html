From two parameters to a surface

How a general sweep is constructed

A sweep needs two curves and a rule for orienting the profile. The path P(u) = (x(u), y(u), z(u)) tells us where to place it. The planar profile C(v) = (a(v), b(v)) tells us its shape. Both parameters range from 0 to 1 independently.

1

Locate the profile

Choose u and evaluate P(u). This is the origin of the moving profile plane. The yellow dashed curve connects these origins.

2

Orient and shape it

Choose orthonormal directions N(u), B(u) for the plane. Rotate them by θ(u), and scale the profile by s(u).

3

Trace the surface

Let v trace the profile while u moves along the path. Connect neighboring samples to obtain the shaded surface and its parameter grid.

S(u, v) = P(u) + s(u) [a(v) Nθ(u) + b(v) Bθ(u)]

Nθ = cos θ N + sin θ B   ·   Bθ = −sin θ N + cos θ B

s(u) = scale × [1 + (taper − 1)u]   ·   θ(u) = u × total twist

Following the path

In transported-frame mode, T = P′/|P′| is the unit tangent. N starts by projecting the global axis least aligned with T into its perpendicular plane; B = T × N. Successive frames use the smallest rotation that aligns their tangents. This keeps the profile perpendicular to the path and avoids the Frenet frame’s instability at straight segments and inflection points.

For a smooth closed path, the accumulated frame rotation is distributed around the loop so the untwisted frame returns to its starting orientation.

Keeping the plane fixed

In fixed-plane mode, N = (1, 0, 0) and B = (0, 1, 0). With zero twist and unit scale, the surface is simply S(u, v) = P(u) + (a(v), b(v), 0). The profile translates without following the path tangent.

The two curves alone do not determine a unique sweep: these different orientation rules produce different surfaces from the same curves.

Try it: choose a circle path and circle profile to form a torus. Replace the profile with an ellipse to see orientation and twist more clearly. A line-segment profile produces a ribbon; a straight path with a circular profile produces a tube.

Seams and regularity. A closed profile makes a tube wall; open ends are not capped. A closed path also needs matching profile position, scale and orientation at the seam. Twist or taper may leave a seam. Large profiles can intersect themselves, and fixed-plane sweeps can collapse locally. Use the self-intersection check to find overlapping non-neighboring triangles in the sampled mesh. Even a completed check cannot certify that the continuous surface is regular or free of intersections between samples.
