From a segment to a closed strip

How the surface is constructed

Let \(L>0\) be the strip width, \(d>L/2\) the loop radius, and \(k\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) the number of half-twists. The original Möbius strip has \(k=1\). Use \(t=L(u-\tfrac12)\), with \(0\le u\le1\), and \(0\le v\le\pi\).

The rotations below are active, right-handed rotations acting on column vectors, as in Geometric Transformations. The first rotation is about the translated z-axis through \((d,0,0)\); the second is about the fixed global y-axis.

i

Form a segment of width L

Take \(A=(0,-L/2,0)\) and \(B=(0,L/2,0)\):

\[L_1(u)=(1-u)A+uB=\boxed{(0,t,0)}.\]

The midpoint \(u=0.5\) is the origin.

ii

Translate by (d, 0, 0)

\[L_2(u)=L_1(u)+(d,0,0)=\boxed{(d,t,0)}.\]

This pure translation places the segment’s midpoint at distance \(d\) from the global y-axis.

iii

Apply k half-twists

Let \(\mathbf a=(d,0,0)\). Rotate about the axis z′ through \(\mathbf a\), parallel to global z, by the angle \(kv\):

\[R_z(kv)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos(kv)&-\sin(kv)&0\\\sin(kv)&\cos(kv)&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.\]
\[S(u,v)=\mathbf a+R_z(kv)\big(L_2(u)-\mathbf a\big)\]
\[\boxed{S(u,v)=(q,t\cos(kv),0)},\qquad q=d-t\sin(kv).\]

When \(k=0\), this leaves the translated line segment unchanged. For \(k\ge1\), the family sweeps a disk of radius \(L/2\) centered at \((d,0,0)\).

iv

Make one circuit about y

\[R_y(2v)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos2v&0&\sin2v\\0&1&0\\-\sin2v&0&\cos2v\end{bmatrix}.\]
\[\boxed{\mathbf r(u,v)=R_y(2v)S(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}q\cos2v\\t\cos(kv)\\-q\sin2v\end{pmatrix}}.\]

Rotate each corresponding ruling through \(2v\) about the global y-axis. As \(v\) goes from 0 to \(\pi\), the loop turns through \(2\pi\) and the width direction turns through \(k\pi\).

The rotation pivot matters: because \(L_2=L_1+\mathbf a\),

\[\mathbf a+R_z(kv)(L_2-\mathbf a)=R_z(kv)L_1+\mathbf a.\]

Translating first and rotating about z′ gives the same result as rotating the original segment about global z and then translating it.

For every twist count, the centerline is \(\mathbf r(\tfrac12,v)=(d\cos2v,0,-d\sin2v)\). It is a circle of radius \(d\). The segment retains its width \(L\) throughout the construction.

Sampling algorithm

  1. Choose \(L>0\), \(d>L/2\), an integer \(k\), and samples \(u_i=i/m\), \(v_j=j\pi/n\).
  2. Form \(L_1(u_i)\), translate by \((d,0,0)\), rotate about z′ through \(kv_j\), then rotate about global y through \(2v_j\).
  3. Connect neighboring samples into quadrilaterals or triangles.
  4. At the seam, join \((u,0)\) to \((1-u,\pi)\) for odd \(k\), or to \((u,\pi)\) for even \(k\).
  5. For a local normal, differentiate \(\mathbf r\) and compute \(\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\).

For \(k\ge1\), the intermediate disk in step iii has a degenerate parameterization at its center: all values of v represent \((d,0,0)\) when \(u=0.5\). The final strip is regular for every twist count shown here.

A local normal

Find the normal at u = 0.5, v = π/2

Write \(C=\cos2v\) and \(S=\sin2v\). Since \(dt/du=L\), the tangent vectors are

\[\mathbf r_u=\big(-L\sin(kv)C,\ L\cos(kv),\ L\sin(kv)S\big),\]
\[\mathbf r_v=\big(-kt\cos(kv)C-2qS,\ -kt\sin(kv),\ kt\cos(kv)S-2qC\big).\]

At \(u=0.5,\ v=\pi/2\), we have \(t=0\), \(q=d\), \(C=-1\), and \(S=0\). Thus

\[\mathbf r=(-d,0,0),\qquad \mathbf r_u=\big(L\sin(k\pi/2),L\cos(k\pi/2),0\big),\qquad \mathbf r_v=(0,0,2d).\]
\[\boxed{\mathbf n=\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v=\big(2Ld\cos(k\pi/2),-2Ld\sin(k\pi/2),0\big)}\]

The corresponding unit normal is

\[\hat{\mathbf n}=\big(\cos(k\pi/2),-\sin(k\pi/2),0\big).\]

Reversing the cross-product order reverses the normal.

Normal for the current width, radius and twist count: (0, −40, 0).

Original Möbius example: for \(k=1\), \(L=2\), and \(d=10\), the point is \((-10,0,0)\) and

\[\mathbf n=(2,0,0)\times(0,0,20)=\boxed{(0,-40,0)}.\]

The seam determines orientability

At the ends of the parameter interval, \(\mathbf r(u,0)=(d,t,0)\) and \(\mathbf r(u,\pi)=(d,(-1)^kt,0)\). Whether the width coordinate reverses depends on the parity of \(k\).

Odd twist counts

For \(k=1\) or \(3\),

\[\mathbf r(u,0)=\mathbf r(1-u,\pi).\]

Join the two vertical edges with opposite directions: the width coordinate reverses at the seam.

Even twist counts: for \(k=0\) or \(2\), join the edges in the same direction, matching the same width coordinate: \(\mathbf r(u,0)=\mathbf r(u,\pi)\).

A closed path, returning or reversed normals

On the centerline, a continuous unit normal along the parameter path is

\[\mathbf N_k(v)=\big(-\cos(kv)\cos2v,-\sin(kv),\cos(kv)\sin2v\big).\]
\[\mathbf N_k(0)=(-1,0,0),\qquad \mathbf N_k(\pi)=\big((-1)^{k+1},0,0\big).\]

The point returns to \((d,0,0)\) after one lap. For odd \(k\), the normal reverses, so these strips are nonorientable. For even \(k\), the normal returns and a consistent global normal exists, so the strips are orientable.

Changing the initial normal’s sign changes both endpoint normals; it does not change whether they agree.

Local regularity and global orientation are different conditions

For every twist count shown here, \(\|\mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v\|^2=L^2(4q^2+k^2t^2)>0\), because \(L>0\) and \(q\ge d-L/2>0\). Thus a local normal exists everywhere.

For odd \(k\), the seam gives \(\mathbf n(u,0)=-\mathbf n(1-u,\pi)\): the local choices cannot form a consistent normal on the joined strip. For even \(k\), \(\mathbf n(u,0)=\mathbf n(u,\pi)\), so the normal agrees across the seam.

For odd \(k\), the edges u = 0 and u = 1 join into one boundary component. For even \(k\), each edge closes on itself, giving two boundary components. The highlighted boundary in the main view follows these edges.

Further reading: Daniel Daners, Unit Normals and Orientation, §9.3, for the continuous-normal definition and the Möbius example. Related course notes: Surfaces and Geometric Transformations.
